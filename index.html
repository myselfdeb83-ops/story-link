Financial Operations and Internal Controls for Vintage Electric

1. End-to-End Supply Chain Cycle

Vintage Electric specializes in electric bicycles. The supply chain cycle involves procuring raw materials, assembling products, and selling through multiple channels. Below is an illustration with numeric examples for a typical electric bicycle (retail price: $2,500).

Supply Chain Stages:

  • Raw Material Procurement: Batteries ($800), Frames ($400), Motors ($300), Tires/Wheels ($200) = Total $1,700
  • Labor Costs for Assembly: $300 per bicycle (2 hours at $150/hour)
  • Overhead and Assembly: $200 per bicycle (factory overhead allocation)
  • Sales: Sold through Fishbowl (wholesale) at $2,000 or Shopify (retail) at $2,500

3. Accounting Treatment

Revenue Recognition:

Revenue is recognized when control transfers to the customer (ASC 606). For retail sales, at point of sale; for wholesale, when shipped.

Expense Recognition:

Expenses matched to revenues using accrual basis. COGM expensed when sold.

Inventory Management:

Valued at lower of cost or market (LCM) using FIFO. Raw materials, WIP, finished goods tracked separately.

Cash and Bank Transactions:

Cash receipts from sales increase cash; payments decrease cash. Bank reconciliations ensure accuracy.

Accounts Receivable:

Recorded at gross amount; allowance for doubtful accounts estimated based on historical data.

Impact on P&L and Balance Sheet:

  • P&L: Revenue increases income; COGS and expenses decrease income.
  • Balance Sheet: Assets: Cash, Inventory, AR increase; Liabilities: AP, Sales Tax Payable increase.

4. Internal Controls and Best Practices

Risks and Controls:

  • Revenue Recognition: Segregation of duties; automated systems to match shipments with invoices.
  • Inventory: Regular physical counts; barcode systems; variance analysis.
  • Sales Tax: Automated tax calculation; quarterly filings; compliance software.
  • Cash Flow: Budgeting; cash flow forecasting; bank reconciliations.
  • AR: Credit checks; aging reports; collection policies.

Best Practices:

Implement SOX-compliant controls; regular audits; use of ERP systems like Fishbowl for integration; training on GAAP compliance.

5. Conclusion

Efficient supply chain and robust controls are crucial for Vintage Electric's financial health. Proper accounting ensures accurate reporting, while controls mitigate risks, supporting sustainable growth and compliance.
